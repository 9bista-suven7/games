      How to play

      The game

      • Dandi Biyo is played in village fields all over Nepal. The biyo is a short wooden peg, tapered at both ends. It lies across the gucchi, a small pit, and the batter hits it with the dandi, a long stick.
      • Hit the biyo as far as you can. Distance is counted in dandi lengths from the gucchi to where the biyo stops; the markers on the ground show every 5.

      Each hit: two taps

      • Flick: the needle swings along the meter. Tap (anywhere on the field, the button, or Space) when it is in the green: the dandi taps the biyo's tapered end and it pops up. The nearer the middle, the higher it pops. Tap in the dark red ends and the flick fails.
      • Hit: while the biyo is in the air (in slow motion), tap again as the ring closes on it. The label tells you how you timed it: Perfect!, Good or Weak. Early hits go higher and to the far side, late hits go lower and to the near side.
      • A higher pop and better timing send it further.

      Loft

      • Drag the Loft lever (or drag up and down on the field, or use ↑/↓) to choose a low drive or a high hit. You can change it until you hit.
      • High hits carry further but hang in the air, so the fielders have time to get under them. Low drives are safer but shorter.

      Lives and the innings

      • You have 3 lives (the ring round your picture). A failed flick, a missed swing, or a catch by a fielder costs a life. Your distances add up until all three are gone.
      • The fielders move before every hit, and move deeper when you hit it long. From the 7th hit a fourth child joins them.
      • The meter speeds up and the slow motion wears off as the innings goes on.

      Modes

      • Solo challenge: one innings. Beat your best total, which is saved on this device.
      • Friends & bots: 2 to 4 players take turns to bat, 1 to 3 innings each, passing the device. Any player can be a bot (Easy, Medium or Hard). Press Skip to jump to the end of a bot's innings. The highest total wins.
